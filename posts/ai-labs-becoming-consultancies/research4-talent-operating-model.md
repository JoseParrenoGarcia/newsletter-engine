## Executive summary

**Yes: frontier AI labs are now hiring roles that look very much like forward-deployed engineering, applied AI deployment, and customer-facing technical implementation.** OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, Scale AI, ServiceNow, Google Cloud, AWS, Sierra and Glean all show evidence of customer-facing AI deployment roles. The clearest signals are OpenAI’s **Forward Deployed Engineer**, Anthropic’s **Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI**, Mistral’s **Forward Deployed Machine Learning Engineer**, Scale AI’s **Forward Deployed Engineer, GenAI**, and ServiceNow’s **Forward Deployed Solution Architect – Applied AI FDE**. These are not just sales roles with AI varnish slapped on, like a consultancy PowerPoint left too close to a GPU. They involve prototypes, production systems, integrations, evals, agents, RAG, workflow redesign, and product feedback loops. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The emerging role category is **explicitly hybrid**: part software engineer, part ML/LLM engineer, part solutions architect, part consultant, part product scout. These roles require coding, LLM implementation, cloud architecture, enterprise security, stakeholder management, ambiguous problem discovery, and the ability to turn customer mess into reusable product patterns. OpenAI says FDEs own “discovery, technical scoping, system design, build, and production rollout”; Anthropic asks FDEs to build production applications, MCP servers, sub-agents, and agent skills; Mistral says its Applied AI team works directly with enterprise clients “from pre-sales through implementation”. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The closest historical analogue is **Palantir’s forward-deployed model**, but the AI-lab version has a different centre of gravity. Palantir’s model was built around embedding engineers to configure and evolve large operational software systems. The new AI FDE model is more centred on **model adoption, agentic workflow design, evaluation, integration into enterprise systems, and feeding product/model gaps back to research and platform teams**. The old thing: customer-facing engineering. The new thing: customer-facing engineering where the “product” is probabilistic, fast-moving, poorly understood by customers, and often not production-ready without substantial local adaptation. Source: https://www.ft.com/content/91002071-7874-4cb7-9245-08ca0571c408?utm_source=chatgpt.com

Consultancies and systems integrators are hiring overlapping roles, but with a different flavour. Accenture, Deloitte, EY, Capgemini, IBM, Wipro, TCS and others emphasise **enterprise architecture, transformation, governance, delivery, operating models, and client-facing implementation**. Capgemini’s Agentic/GenAI Consultant role, for example, combines full-stack application delivery, agentic workflows, human-in-the-loop controls, auditability, monitoring, and client-facing delivery. IBM’s AI Integration Consultant description highlights RAG pipelines, prompts, guardrails, APIs, orchestration, observability, tuning and cost optimisation. Source: https://careers.capgemini.com/job/London-Agentic-GenAI-Consultant-Senior-Consultant/1285911901/

The labour-market implication is clear: **data scientists and ML engineers who can only model offline are less differentiated than those who can deploy, evaluate, integrate, explain, and influence adoption.** The premium role is becoming the person who can move from “we have an LLM demo” to “this is a secure, monitored, governed workflow that people actually use”. That is not exactly traditional data science, not exactly software engineering, not exactly consulting. It is annoyingly all of them. Source: https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-engineer-large-enterprise-london-uk/

## Common skill patterns

The most common pattern is **full-stack technical delivery plus LLM-specific implementation**. The roles repeatedly ask for Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, backend/frontend ability, APIs, cloud architecture, and production-grade software engineering. On top of that, the AI-specific layer includes LLM behaviour, prompt engineering, RAG, agent frameworks, evals, fine-tuning, model serving, observability, cost/performance trade-offs, and security/governance. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The second pattern is **customer-facing ambiguity management**. These roles are not handed neat Jira tickets by a serene product manager floating down from Mount Roadmap. They are expected to discover messy workflows, identify high-value use cases, scope prototypes, make technical trade-offs, work with executives, and guide adoption. OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, ServiceNow and Accenture all explicitly mention stakeholder communication, executive audiences, customer discovery, technical advisory, or business value translation. Source: https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-engineer-large-enterprise-london-uk/

The third pattern is **productionisation, not just demos**. The strongest job descriptions distinguish themselves from “cool prototype theatre”. OpenAI FDEs own delivery from prototype to stable production; AWS wants systems moved from intent to production-ready environments; ServiceNow defines success as an AI solution-ready build that becomes the foundation for scaled production deployment; Capgemini talks about MVPs, secure scalable production-grade solutions, and evaluation/monitoring for regulated clients. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The fourth pattern is **feedback loops back into product and research**. This is particularly important for frontier AI labs. OpenAI says FDEs provide field feedback to Research and Product; Anthropic says FDEs codify repeatable deployment patterns and contribute insights back to Product and Engineering; Mistral says Applied AI collaborates with product and science teams to improve product and model capabilities from customer feedback. This is more than implementation work. It is field learning as product discovery. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The fifth pattern is **reusable artefact creation**. The recurring language is “playbooks”, “reference architectures”, “deployment patterns”, “technical documentation”, “tools”, “building blocks”, “practice IP”, and “codified for scale”. That suggests these organisations do not want bespoke consulting forever. They want repeated customer work to crystallise into reusable implementation patterns, platform improvements, or scalable GTM assets. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

## What is new versus old wine in new bottles?

### Old wine

A lot of this is recognisably old. Cloud providers have long had **solutions architects**, **customer engineers**, **technical account managers**, and **professional services engineers**. Consultancies have long had **technical consultants**, **enterprise architects**, and **transformation leads**. Enterprise software companies have long used pre-sales engineers, post-sales implementation teams, and customer success organisations to turn complex platforms into working deployments. AWS and Google Cloud roles show this clearly: they are customer-facing technical advisory roles helping clients use cloud and AI services in production. Source: https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/10399209/genai-ml-specialist-solutions-architect-aws-global-sales-ags

Consultancies are also not suddenly discovering that technology must be integrated into workflows. Accenture’s Data & AI Architect role is classic systems-integration language: multi-platform architecture, delivery leadership, integration patterns, CRM/CDP/CMS/commerce systems, and client stakeholder alignment. Capgemini’s role is very much a consulting delivery role, including proposals, RFPs, business development, and internal practice development. Source: https://www.accenture.com/gb-en/careers/jobdetails?id=R00322202_en

### New bottles

The new part is the **probabilistic AI deployment layer**. These roles are not just integrating deterministic SaaS platforms. They are deploying systems where behaviour can vary, evals matter, prompts and policies become artefacts, and production quality depends on monitoring non-deterministic outputs. Anthropic’s explicit references to agent development, eval frameworks, MCP servers, sub-agents, and agent skills are especially telling. Capgemini’s mention of HITL gates, auditability, policy controls, and robust eval/monitoring for regulated clients points in the same direction. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/careers/jobs/4985877008

Another new part is the **collapse of boundaries between model provider and implementer**. Historically, a model/API vendor could sell infrastructure or software and rely on SIs or customers to implement. But the OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral role descriptions show model providers moving closer to the deployment surface. They are not just providing APIs. They are sending technical staff into customer environments to build applications, shape workflows, gather feedback, and improve products. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

A third new part is **agentic workflow design as a first-class skill**. Multiple roles explicitly mention agents, agentic workflows, orchestration, MCP, AgentOps, human-in-the-loop controls, and evaluation. This is distinct from traditional ML deployment, where the central challenge was often model training, feature pipelines, serving, and monitoring. Here, the work increasingly includes designing a semi-autonomous workflow that can use tools, access enterprise context, respect policies, and produce auditable outputs. Source: https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/3176458/genai-solution-architect

## Comparison with Palantir’s forward-deployed engineering model

The comparison with Palantir is not speculative fluff. The phrase **forward-deployed engineer** itself is widely associated with Palantir, and recent reporting explicitly frames OpenAI, Anthropic and Cohere’s hiring as borrowing or adapting that model for generative AI. The Financial Times reported that Anthropic, OpenAI and Cohere were recruiting FDEs as a new job for generative AI companies, with OpenAI setting up an FDE team in 2025 and Anthropic planning to grow its Applied AI team substantially. Source: https://www.ft.com/content/91002071-7874-4cb7-9245-08ca0571c408?utm_source=chatgpt.com

The similarities are strong. Both models embed technical people close to customer problems. Both value people who can code and talk to stakeholders. Both use field work to uncover what customers actually need rather than what a central product team assumes they need. Both try to convert messy operational pain into working systems and reusable product patterns. OpenAI’s FDE role, for example, looks very Palantir-like in its end-to-end ownership of discovery, scoping, system design, build and rollout. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The differences matter too. Palantir historically built around data integration, operational workflows, analytics platforms, and mission-critical deployments. The frontier AI version adds model capability uncertainty, prompt/eval loops, agent design, safety, governance, and rapid model evolution. An OpenAI FDE might discover that the model is not reliable enough for a workflow, feed that back to product or research, design evals, and build mitigations. That is not identical to deploying a traditional enterprise platform. It is more like deploying a platform while the laws of physics are still being patched every Thursday. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

There is also a commercial difference. In classic SaaS, professional services can be treated as support for software revenue. In frontier AI, these roles may also be **demand creation**, **use-case discovery**, **enterprise trust-building**, and **token/API consumption acceleration**. If customers do not know how to get from prototype to production, the model provider has a reason to send in engineers who unblock adoption. Business Insider reported OpenAI executives describing FDEs as a way to move large customer projects into production and scale, with the model tested on pilot customers. Source: https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-forward-deployed-engineers-accelerate-ai-projects-2025-7?utm_source=chatgpt.com

## What this means for data scientists and ML engineers

For data scientists, the signal is uncomfortable but useful: **modelling alone is becoming less of a moat**. The scarce skill is increasingly the ability to connect models to workflows, systems, users, governance and measurable outcomes. The roles above reward people who can combine ML/LLM understanding with production engineering and stakeholder fluency. Anthropic asks for LLM production experience, prompt engineering, agent development and eval frameworks; Mistral wants ML/product implementation, APIs, front-end/back-end interfaces, fine-tuning, RAG and agents; AWS wants AgentOps, observability, model-serving and enterprise integration. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/careers/jobs/4985877008

For ML engineers, the role boundary is expanding outward. It is no longer only about training pipelines, serving infrastructure, latency, monitoring and data quality. Those still matter. But the frontier deployment role also needs workflow analysis, product sense, eval design, security awareness, executive communication, and the courage to say: “This should not be an agent. It should be a form.” A small mercy for civilisation, really. Source: https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-engineer-large-enterprise-london-uk/

For analytics engineers and data engineers, the opportunity is stronger than it may first appear. Enterprise AI deployment is often constrained less by models and more by context: messy permissions, fragmented systems, unreliable knowledge stores, weak metadata, missing observability, and no clear source of truth. Roles at ServiceNow, IBM, Capgemini and TCS highlight data catalogues, enterprise integrations, RAG pipelines, orchestration, governance and AI-ready infrastructure. Source: https://careers.servicenow.com/jobs/744000127045629/solution-architect-ai-data/

For product engineers, this is a potential career accelerant. Many FDE-like roles are essentially product engineering in compressed, high-context environments: build prototypes, watch customer failure modes, turn repeated patterns into reusable components, and influence the roadmap. OpenAI, Anthropic, Scale AI and Mistral all explicitly mention product feedback, roadmap influence, or repeatable deployment patterns. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

The likely winners are **T-shaped builders**: deep enough technically to ship production systems, broad enough to understand business workflows, and pragmatic enough to avoid turning every problem into a LangChain cathedral. The people who can translate between “C-suite transformation roadmap” and “this API times out under load” will be annoyingly valuable. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/careers/jobs/4977624008

## What this means for companies adopting AI

Companies adopting AI should expect the best vendors to bring more than an API key. The emerging operating model includes **technical discovery, architecture design, prototype delivery, eval frameworks, security/compliance guidance, workflow redesign, and production rollout support**. That is visible in OpenAI’s AI Deployment Engineer role, Anthropic’s Applied AI Architect role, AWS’s GenAI/ML Specialist Solutions Architect role, and ServiceNow’s AI & Data Solution Architect role. Source: https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-engineer-large-enterprise-london-uk/

However, companies should be careful not to outsource organisational learning entirely. FDEs and AI deployment engineers can accelerate adoption, but they can also create dependency if internal teams do not learn how the systems work. The critical internal capabilities are eval ownership, data governance, security review, workflow ownership, product management, and maintenance. Otherwise, the vendor builds the clever thing, everyone claps, and six months later no one knows why the clever thing occasionally emails a spreadsheet to the wrong goblin. Source: https://careers.servicenow.com/jobs/744000131533810/forward-deployed-solution-architect-applied-ai-fde/

Companies should also distinguish three vendor promises. First, **pre-sales advisory**: useful for understanding possibilities, but not enough for deployment. Second, **implementation support**: useful for getting specific use cases live. Third, **operating-model transformation**: useful only if paired with internal ownership, governance and process redesign. The roles from Anthropic, OpenAI, ServiceNow, Accenture and Capgemini span all three, but different job families emphasise different parts of the journey. Source: https://openai.com/careers/solutions-engineer-pre-sales/

A practical implication: procurement should not only ask, “Which model is best?” It should also ask, “Who will help us deploy it? Who owns evals? Who owns security sign-off? Who maintains prompts, tools and integrations? What becomes reusable? What happens when the model changes?” The labour-market shift is telling us that deployment, not model access, is becoming the expensive bottleneck. Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

## Is “forward-deployed AI engineer” becoming an important new role category?

**Yes, but with naming fragmentation.** The same family of work appears under many titles: Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, Forward Deployed ML Engineer, AI Deployment Engineer, AI Deployment Strategist, GenAI Solution Architect, AI Outcomes Manager, Agent Engineer, Agent Strategist, AI Integration Consultant, and Agentic/GenAI Consultant. The title is unstable; the role pattern is increasingly clear. Source: https://openai.com/careers/search/?q=forward+deployed+engineer&utm_source=chatgpt.com

The strongest evidence comes from companies creating explicit teams and job families around this work. OpenAI’s careers search shows multiple FDE roles across geographies; Anthropic has an Applied AI team with FDEs and architects; Mistral has an entire Solutions category full of AI Deployment Strategists and Applied AI/FDE roles; ServiceNow explicitly references a growing Applied AI Forward Deployed Engineering team. Source: https://openai.com/careers/search/?q=forward+deployed+engineer&utm_source=chatgpt.com

The role category is likely to matter because it sits at the highest-friction point in enterprise AI: the gap between generic model capability and specific business impact. That gap is not solved by better benchmarks alone. It is solved by people who can understand the workflow, build the integration, design the eval, manage the risk, persuade the stakeholders, and decide when the answer is not “more AI” but “fix the data pipeline first”. Source: https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-engineer-large-enterprise-london-uk/

## Open questions and weak evidence

The first weak point is **job-posting evidence can exaggerate strategic intent**. A company may list several FDE-like roles because it is experimenting with a GTM motion, not because the operating model is mature. Google’s search result lists a “Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud”, but the job detail page returned “job not found” in the crawl, so that evidence should be marked as weaker than OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, Scale AI or ServiceNow, where full job descriptions were accessible. Source: https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/119845154621137606-customer-engineer-platform-and-ai-google-cloud

The second open question is **whether this scales economically**. FDE work is labour-intensive. If every enterprise AI deployment requires elite engineers embedded with customers, margins may look more like consulting than software. Reporting already reflects some scepticism: Business Insider covered criticism from former Snowflake CRO Chris Degnan, who described FDEs as “glorified professional services” and questioned whether top engineers want these roles. That is commentary, not settled fact, but it highlights the tension. Source: https://www.businessinsider.com/snowflake-cro-forward-deployed-engineers-ai-job-2026-5?utm_source=chatgpt.com

The third open question is **what happens after the prototype**. Some roles promise production deployment, but maintaining AI workflows over time is harder than launching them. Model behaviour changes, workflows drift, data changes, security policies evolve, and users discover wonderfully inventive ways to break systems. Job descriptions mention observability, evals, governance and production readiness, but public evidence is still thin on long-term maintenance models. Source: https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/3176458/genai-solution-architect

The fourth open question is **how much frontier labs will partner with consultancies versus compete with them**. Consultancies still have scale, industry relationships, procurement access, and transformation muscles. Frontier labs have model knowledge, product leverage, and direct insight into model limitations. The likely near-term answer is “both”: labs deploy FDEs for strategic accounts and repeatable patterns, while consultancies and SIs handle broader transformation, migration, governance and long-tail implementation. But the boundary is moving. Source: https://www.accenture.com/gb-en/careers/jobdetails?id=R00322202_en

The fifth open question is **whether these jobs become prestigious career tracks or difficult hybrid roles that burn people out**. Reporting from the FT and Business Insider suggests demand has surged, but also that the work can be demanding and contested in status relative to core product engineering. It may become a high-impact path for builders who enjoy ambiguity, travel, stakeholder contact and production pressure. It may be a terrible fit for engineers who want quiet focus and beautifully bounded problem statements. Rare creatures, those bounded problem statements. Source: https://www.ft.com/content/91002071-7874-4cb7-9245-08ca0571c408?utm_source=chatgpt.com

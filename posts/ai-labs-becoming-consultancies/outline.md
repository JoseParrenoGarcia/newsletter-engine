### 2. What is a forward-deployed engineer — and where does the model come from?
- Define the FDE role concretely: what they do, how engagements work, how they differ from traditional consulting (embed alongside the customer's team; feed patterns back to the platform).
- Trace the model to Palantir: AIP Bootcamp, the decade-long maturation, why the "last mile" is longer than it looks.
- Key angle: this is not a novel invention. It is a well-worn enterprise software playbook. The question is whether labs can run it at scale.
- Sources: [Palantir — AIP Bootcamp](https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/bootcamp/), [MarketWatch — Palantir pioneered the hottest job in tech; copycats may not succeed](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/palantir-pioneered-the-hottest-job-in-tech-its-legions-of-copycats-may-not-succeed-bdd581e3), [WSJ — AI startups' new old secret weapon: forward deployed engineers](https://www.wsj.com/articles/ai-startups-have-a-new-old-secret-weapon-forward-deployed-engineers-d18ee609), [Business Insider — OpenAI forward deployed engineers accelerate AI projects](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-forward-deployed-engineers-accelerate-ai-projects-2025-7)

### 3. How are the two labs approaching this differently?
- OpenAI: vertically ambitious — owns DeployCo, Frontier platform, explicit Alliances with McKinsey/BCG/Accenture/Capgemini, Tomoro acquisition seeds its own FDE headcount.
- Anthropic: ecosystem-led — PE investors (Blackstone, Apollo, Goldman) are distribution infrastructure into their portfolio companies; mass-trains partner workforces (30k Accenture professionals, 470k Deloitte network) rather than building its own delivery headcount at scale.
- Key angle: one is building a vertical stack; the other is building a channel. Neither strategy is clearly dominant yet — these are capital commitments, not proven margins.
- Sources: [OpenAI — Introducing OpenAI Frontier](https://openai.com/index/introducing-openai-frontier/), [Anthropic — Accenture partnership](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-accenture-partnership), [Anthropic — Deloitte partnership](https://www.anthropic.com/news/deloitte-anthropic-partnership), [TechCrunch — OpenAI calls in the consultants for its enterprise push](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/openai-calls-in-the-consultants-for-its-enterprise-push/)

### 4. Who are they targeting — and who are they deliberately not?
- Primary targets: large enterprise, regulated industries (finance, healthcare, government), PE-backed portfolio companies.
- Anthropic explicitly positions the new services company for mid-sized companies — a deliberate concession to partner GSIs who own large enterprise relationships.
- Not targeting: individual DS teams, mid-market organisations without large IT budgets, internal DS functions that already run well.
- Key angle: the threat to "your data team" is not direct. These are $300M+ deployments with PE co-investors and 150+ FDEs. That is not your context. But the ecosystem shift matters anyway.
- Sources: [Anthropic — Enterprise AI Services Company](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-ai-services-company), [Anthropic — Claude for Financial Services](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-financial-services), [Accenture — $3bn AI investment (newsroom)](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2023/accenture-to-invest-3-billion-in-ai-to-accelerate-clients-reinvention)

### 5. Why does the consulting layer always return?
- ERP: SAP and Oracle required armies of consultants to deploy; no organisation went live on time without one. Implementation costs routinely exceeded software licence costs.
- RPA: Deloitte's intelligent automation survey documented that ROI materialises mainly for organisations that move beyond piloting. Most did not — and the ones that did used delivery partners.
- Cloud migration: AWS MAP and Google Cloud Migration Programs are formalised partner-delivery infrastructure; cloud providers could not move enterprise customers without them.
- Key angle: "capability is not deployment." The hard part has never been the tool — it is the organisation around the tool. AI is not different; it is more intense.
- Sources: [McKinsey — Getting an ERP transformation back on track](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/getting-an-erp-transformation-back-on-track), [Deloitte — Intelligent automation / RPA survey](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/intelligent-automation-2022-survey-results.html), [AWS — Migration Acceleration Program](https://aws.amazon.com/migration-acceleration-program/), [UiPath — RPA Centre of Excellence](https://www.uipath.com/rpa/center-of-excellence)

### 6. Is this a scalable product-learning loop or just another expensive human layer?
- The honest economic question. Salesforce FY2025: subscription and support is ~94% of revenue; professional services is deliberately a cost-of-sale. ServiceNow FY2025: professional services had a gross loss of 5%. Services are adoption infrastructure, not the profit engine.
- The senior-engineer opportunity cost: every FDE embedded with one enterprise customer is not building core model capability. This compounds.
- The Palantir comparison: it took >10 years to mature the FDE model into repeatable margins. Labs are earlier on that curve.
- Key angle: the question is not "are labs becoming consultancies?" but "can they build the FDE model into a scalable product-learning loop before the economics of embedding senior engineers drag them under?"
- Sources: [Palantir — 10-K FY2025](https://www.moomoo.com/news/notice/306500427/palantir-10-k-fy2025-annual-report), [ServiceNow — Q4 & Full Year 2025 results](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results-Board-of-Directors-Authorizes-Additional-5B-for-Share-Repurchase-Program/default.aspx), [BCG — Rethinking B2B software pricing in the era of AI](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/rethinking-b2b-software-pricing-in-the-era-of-ai), [FT — AI deployment staffing](https://www.ft.com/content/91002071-7874-4cb7-9245-08ca0571c408)

### 7. What should DS and tech leaders take from this?
- **Takeaway 1 — The forward-deployed solutions path is a real career.** The FDE role is proliferating across labs, cloud providers, GSIs, and enterprise software companies. Titles vary wildly (AI Deployment Engineer, Applied AI Architect, Agent Strategist). The job is: deep enough to build, broad enough to understand business workflows, willing to live in customer environments. This is a differentiated role that DS leaders should be watching — for hiring, for career planning, and for understanding what skills compound in this market.
- **Takeaway 2 — Your data team is not the target.** Labs are deploying against large enterprise, regulated industries, and PE portfolio companies with nine-figure budgets. They are not coming for the internal DS team at a 500-person company. The risk is subtler: if labs deploy successfully at the top of the market, the patterns they codify become the next wave of self-serve tooling. You will feel this in 3–5 years, not in 3–5 months.
- **Takeaway 3 — Study the model, not just the announcement.** The FDE playbook — embed, build, feed back, codify — is a template for how any DS or data function should be thinking about internal deployment. Not "what AI tool can we buy?" but "who owns evals, who owns prompt maintenance, what happens when the model updates?" The labs are showing you the operating model for AI deployment at scale. That model applies inside your org too.
- Sources: [FT — AI deployment staffing](https://www.ft.com/content/91002071-7874-4cb7-9245-08ca0571c408), [McKinsey — State of AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai), model knowledge for DS leadership framing

# Frontier AI labs and enterprise AI deployment services

## Executive summary

**The factual situation has moved beyond speculation.** Both OpenAI and Anthropic have now publicly announced structures that look like an enterprise AI deployment layer: hands-on engineering, partner networks, systems integrators, workflow redesign, and long-term production support.

**OpenAI has officially launched the OpenAI Deployment Company**, described as a new company that helps organisations “build and deploy AI systems” across important work. It is majority-owned and controlled by OpenAI, launches with **more than $4 billion** of initial investment, includes **19 investment, consulting, and systems-integration partners**, and is acquiring **Tomoro**, an applied AI consulting and engineering firm, bringing around **150 Forward Deployed Engineers and Deployment Specialists** from day one.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

**Anthropic has officially announced a new enterprise AI services company** with **Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs**, aimed at bringing Claude into core operations at mid-sized companies. Anthropic says Applied AI engineers will work alongside the new company’s engineering teams to build tailored Claude-powered systems.

Source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-ai-services-company

**The pattern is clear:** frontier labs are not simply selling APIs or chatbots. They are building delivery capacity around models. That does not make them “consultancies” in the old McKinsey/Accenture sense, but they are definitely converging with parts of the enterprise technology services model: implementation, change management, workflow redesign, certification, partner ecosystems, and production support. Very Silicon Valley: reinvent consulting, but with better GPUs and a more expensive hoodie.

## Exact language used by the companies and partners

### OpenAI

OpenAI uses the following terms repeatedly:

#### “Deployment Company”

OpenAI’s official title is “OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company to help businesses build around intelligence”.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

#### “Build and deploy AI systems”

OpenAI says the new company is designed to help organisations “build and deploy AI systems they can rely on every day”.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

#### “Forward Deployed Engineers”

OpenAI says the company will embed engineers specialised in frontier AI deployment, known as FDEs, into organisations. Its FDE job postings say these engineers lead discovery, technical scoping, system design, build, and production rollout.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

#### “Redesign organisational infrastructure and critical workflows”

OpenAI says FDEs will work with business leaders, operators, and frontline teams to identify AI impact, redesign infrastructure and workflows, and turn gains into durable systems.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

#### “Frontier Alliances”

OpenAI uses this term for partnerships with BCG, McKinsey, Accenture, and Capgemini. These partners help define strategy, integrate systems, redesign workflows, and scale deployment globally.

Source: https://openai.com/index/frontier-alliance-partners/

#### “AI coworkers” and “agents”

OpenAI describes Frontier as a platform for building, deploying, and managing AI coworkers that can do work across the enterprise.

Source: https://openai.com/index/frontier-alliance-partners/

## What we can safely say

1. **OpenAI and Anthropic are both building enterprise deployment capacity beyond API access.**

   OpenAI’s Deployment Company and Anthropic’s enterprise AI services company both focus on hands-on implementation, workflow integration, and production systems.

   Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

2. **Both companies are using partner ecosystems rather than trying to deliver everything alone.**

   OpenAI uses Frontier Alliances and named consulting/SI partners. Anthropic uses the Claude Partner Network and major relationships with Accenture, Deloitte, PwC, TCS, and others.

   Source: https://openai.com/index/frontier-alliance-partners/

3. **Forward-deployed engineering is now a central enterprise AI operating model.**

   OpenAI and Anthropic both have FDE roles focused on embedding with customers, building production systems, shaping deployment patterns, and feeding customer learnings back into product and engineering.

   Source: https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-engineer-london-london-uk/

4. **The labs are moving closer to professional-services territory, but not exactly becoming traditional consultancies.**

   They are not merely selling decks and strategy work. Their model is more technical: embedded engineers, model/platform access, production applications, enterprise data integration, evals, governance, and repeatable deployment patterns.

5. **Alternative asset managers and private equity are becoming distribution partners, not just investors.**

   OpenAI names TPG, Advent, Bain Capital, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, Warburg Pincus, and others. Anthropic names Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, Goldman Sachs, Apollo, General Atlantic, GIC, Leonard Green, and Sequoia. The strategic logic is access to large portfolios of companies that need AI deployment.

   Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

## What we should not overclaim

1. **Do not say OpenAI or Anthropic have “become consultancies”.**

   The better phrasing is: they are building *consulting-like deployment capacity* around frontier AI, often through partner networks and new service entities.

2. **Do not assume all reported valuations are official.**

   OpenAI officially confirms more than $4bn initial investment, but not the $10bn valuation in the official page I found.

   Anthropic confirms the new company and partners, but not the $1.5bn valuation or $300m commitments in its announcement. Those remain reported figures.

   Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

3. **Do not claim the OpenAI venture was only rumoured.**

   Earlier press reporting existed, but OpenAI has now officially announced the OpenAI Deployment Company.

   Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/

4. **Do not conflate OpenAI Deployment Company with SB OpenAI Japan.**

   SB OpenAI Japan is a Japan-focused JV with SoftBank around Cristal intelligence. OpenAI Deployment Company is a broader enterprise deployment company majority-owned and controlled by OpenAI.

   Source: https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250203_0

5. **Do not assume “FDE” means the same operating model everywhere.**

   OpenAI, Anthropic, and Accenture use overlapping language, but exact delivery structures, incentives, margins, customer ownership, and product feedback loops may differ.

6. **Do not assume enterprise AI deployment has been proven economically at scale.**

   The announcements show strategic commitment and investment. They do not yet prove long-term margins, repeatability, customer ROI, or whether these models avoid becoming bespoke services businesses.

## Open questions for follow-up research

1. **Unit economics:** Are these deployment companies expected to behave like high-margin software distribution, lower-margin services, or something hybrid?

2. **Customer ownership:** Who owns the customer relationship: the lab, the deployment company, the consulting partner, or the investor-backed service entity?

3. **Product feedback loop:** How much do FDE deployments feed back into OpenAI and Anthropic model/product roadmaps?

4. **Governance and liability:** Who is accountable when a deployed agent fails in a regulated workflow?

5. **Partner conflict:** How do labs avoid competing with Accenture, Deloitte, PwC, McKinsey, Capgemini, and similar firms while also building their own deployment arms?

6. **Portfolio-company distribution:** How much of the investment logic depends on PE and asset-manager portfolio companies becoming customers?

7. **Moat durability:** Is the moat the model, the deployment capability, the partner ecosystem, the workflow data, or the customer lock-in?

8. **Comparison to Palantir:** Are OpenAI and Anthropic genuinely adopting a Palantir-style FDE model, or just borrowing some language and practices?

9. **International structures:** Will SB OpenAI Japan become a template for other country-specific AI deployment JVs?

10. **Regulated industries:** Healthcare, financial services, public sector, and life sciences appear repeatedly. Which verticals produce the first durable proof points?

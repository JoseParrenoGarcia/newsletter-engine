## Brainstorm Summary

The post takes a concrete news hook — both OpenAI and Anthropic have built formal deployment capacity in 2025–2026 — and uses it to make a strategic argument for DS and tech leaders. OpenAI launched a Deployment Company with $4B+ investment, ~150 Forward Deployed Engineers via the Tomoro acquisition, and explicit Frontier Alliances with McKinsey, BCG, Accenture, and Capgemini. Anthropic formed a separate enterprise AI services company backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs, and is distributing through PE portfolio companies while mass-training partner workforces (30k Accenture professionals, 470k Deloitte employees).

The surface read — "labs becoming consultancies" — is less interesting than the divergence beneath it. OpenAI looks vertically ambitious: it wants to own the enterprise engagement end-to-end. Anthropic looks ecosystem-led: it is using PE investors as distribution infrastructure into their portfolio companies and leaning on partner workforces rather than building its own. Neither has proven the economics at scale. The research is explicit that these are capital commitments and intent signals, not proven margins or repeatable delivery.

The historical lens is important. Every major enterprise technology wave — ERP, Salesforce, RPA, cloud migration — required a large human implementation layer before value arrived. The consulting layer keeps returning because capability is not deployment, and the hard part is rarely the tool; it is the organisation around the tool. The key question is whether labs' deployment teams become a scalable product-learning loop (FDE feedback feeds model improvement and reusable patterns) or merely the latest expensive human layer. The economics from Salesforce and ServiceNow are instructive: professional services carry lower or negative gross margins and are treated as adoption infrastructure, not a profit engine.

The post closes with three takeaways for the leader: (1) the forward-deployed solutions professional is a real emerging career path worth understanding; (2) your data team is not the target — labs are going after large enterprise, regulated industries, and PE-backed portfolios, not the mid-market or individual DS functions; (3) this ecosystem shift is a signal about where the broader industry is heading, and leaders should be studying the model, not fearing it.

The tone is serious and sourced — named sources, real numbers, and chart/article placeholders throughout. The primary reference is the "open source models" post: same audience, same strategic register, same habit of grounding claims before drawing conclusions.

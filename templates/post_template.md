# Post Template — Universal Structural Skeleton

This template applies to all post types. Type-specific style guides extend or override individual elements.

---

## Intro

**Personal anecdote → framing → thesis.**

- Open with a specific, real scene — not a hypothetical. A commute, a difficult conversation, a moment of recognition.
- The anecdote establishes emotional credibility before any framework or advice.
- End the intro with an explicit thesis statement before the first `##` heading.

*Exception: `series-genai` posts open with a thesis or contrarian reframe, not an anecdote. See `style_guide/types/series-genai.md`.*

*Exception: `paper-explainer` posts open with paper attribution, not an anecdote. See `style_guide/types/paper-explainer.md`.*

---

## Preview section

**Placed directly after the intro, before the first content `##` heading.**

Heading (any variant acceptable):
- `## What will we cover?`
- `## What will we cover in this post?`
- `## What will we cover in this blog post?`
- `## What this post covers`

Format: labelled bullet list, 4–7 bullets, one per major section.

```
- **Bold label.** One to two sentence explainer of what will be covered and why it matters.
```

Purpose: orients the reader before the argument begins. For longer posts (10+ min), this is the reader's map.

---

## Main body

**5–8 `##` sections.**

- Headings are noun-phrase or verb-phrase declarations — not questions or imperatives.
- Each section advances the argument.
- Sections should feel inevitable: each one answers the natural question the previous section raises.
- Close each section with a claim or observation that implies the next question. Open the next section by answering it directly. No meta-commentary transitions ("Now that we've explored X...").

---

## Closing thoughts

**Named `##` section — not the final paragraphs of a content section.**

Heading (any variant acceptable):
- `## Closing thoughts`
- `## Summary`
- `## Final thoughts: <specific topic>`
- `## Wrapping it up: <specific topic>`

Synthesises the argument and connects back to the opening anecdote and thesis. Not a bullet-point summary of what was covered.

---

## Now, I want to hear from you

**Named `##` section. Always the last section before References.**

2–4 specific questions tied to the post's argument. Not a generic sign-off.

- Do: bullet questions tied to the post's specific argument
- Do: a short paragraph naming what you are curious about, followed by 2–3 targeted questions
- Avoid: "Let me know your thoughts in the comments."
